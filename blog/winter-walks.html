Winter walks are the best walks (yes, really)

Somewhere around the second week of December, every year, a certain kind of dog owner decides walks are a summer activity and the yard will do. I used to be that owner. Then I got a dog who disagreed, loudly, at 6:40 every morning.

What winter gives you

Empty streets, for one. In summer the park is chaos of strollers and scooters. In winter you get the whole place, and a dog on a quiet trail sniffing frost is a different animal than the same dog navigating summer crowds.

Cold also sharpens scent. Many dogs are simply more engaged in cold air. Mine will spend ten minutes on one frozen grass patch, reading the neighborhood news, while in August he gives it a two-second glance and moves on.

The gear part, briefly

You do not need much. Paws are the weak point for most dogs, not the body. Salt and ice are the actual enemies: they sting, they crack pads, and city salt is mildly toxic when licked off.

A thin paw wax or a simple bootie solves most of it. Short-coated dogs, seniors, and small breeds often do want a jacket. Watch the dog, not the thermometer. Shivering, paw-lifting, and a hunched back mean it is time to head home.

For reflective gear, winter is when it earns its price. Dark mornings and dark evenings mean your dog is far less visible than you think. A stitch of reflective thread on a harness catches headlights from much further away than a bright color does. We learned this the hard way in our early samples and now it is a standard detail on everything we make for winter markets.

Give winter walks one honest try. Pick the coldest, clearest morning you can, take a thermos, and let the dog set the pace. You might find, as I did, that it becomes the best part of the day.

Is it safe to walk my dog in winter?

Yes with the right gear. Protect paws from salt and ice with wax or booties, watch for shivering and paw-lifting, and add a jacket for short-coated, senior or small dogs.

What should my dog wear in cold weather?

Short-coated, senior and small breeds often need a jacket; most dogs need paw protection from salt and ice. Use reflective gear on dark winter mornings and evenings.
