Puppy chewing season: what actually worked

Between four and seven months, our puppy destroyed: one slipper, two chair legs, a phone charger, the corner of a rug, and a hardcover novel, which he seemed to savor. This is not a cautionary tale. It is a season, and like weather, you prepare for it rather than resent it.

Why puppies chew like it is their job

Because it is. Adult teeth coming in hurt, and chewing relieves the pressure. Then, right when teething ends, the real reason arrives: puppy adolescence meets the fact that puppies explore the world with their mouths. Chewing is also self-soothing, like nail-biting for a creature with no nails.

Punishing chewing teaches nothing useful. The drive is chemical. Your job is to give it a place to go.

The three-part system that saved our sofa

One, rotation. Ten toys out is zero toys. We kept three or four available and swapped weekly so everything stayed interesting. Frozen was best on sore-gum days: a wet rope toy from the freezer, or a stuffed rubber toy frozen overnight.

Two, trade, never chase. Chasing a puppy with a slipper makes the slipper a prize. Trading for something better makes dropping things a habit. Our dog would eventually spit out contraband on sight, expecting an upgrade. That habit saved his life more than once, on street-floor findings.

Three, confinement you can trust. A pen or a puppy-proofed room when nobody could watch him. Management is not cheating; it is how the shoes survive long enough for the habit work to stick.

A note from the product side

Chewing season is also how we test. A sample that survives a teething lab puppy for a month has earned its place in the catalog. Several of our toughest toy designs exist because of that one winter, chosen by a puppy who was extremely good at his job.

The season ends. The novel was replaced. The chair legs survived with minor scarring, and the dog, now grown, has not touched a slipper in years. Hang in there and hide the chargers.

Why does my puppy chew everything?

Teething and mouth exploration drive it, usually between four and seven months. It is a phase, not bad behavior. Redirect chewing to appropriate toys.

How do I stop puppy chewing?

Rotate a few toys, trade (do not chase) for something better, and use a puppy-proofed area when unsupervised. Frozen toys help sore gums.
