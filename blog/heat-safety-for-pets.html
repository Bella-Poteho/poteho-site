Heat safety for pets: the talk we have every summer

Every summer, without fail, the same preventable things happen. The dog in the parked car. The shortened walk at noon. The bulldog who was fine right up until he was not. So this is the talk, the one we give customers, friends, and occasionally strangers in parking lots.

The car, once and forever

A parked car is an oven on a timer. On a 24-degree day, the inside of a car can pass 38 degrees within ten minutes. Cracked windows change almost nothing. There is no safe duration, no shade, no quick errand. The dog stays home. That is the whole rule and it has no exceptions.

Walking in heat

Try the pavement test with the back of your hand: seven seconds on the asphalt. If you cannot hold it, it burns paw pads. Early morning and late evening are the walking windows in summer; midday walks are for January.

Brachycephalic breeds, meaning the flat-faced crew like bulldogs, pugs, and Persians on the cat side, overheat at temperatures other dogs shrug off. So do overweight dogs, seniors, and thick-coated breeds. For them, summer walks are shorter, slower, and earlier than your schedule prefers. Their cooling system is simply built with a narrower margin.

Know the emergency signs: heavy panting that turns weak, bright red gums, wobbliness, vomiting, collapse. That is heatstroke, and it is a straight-to-vet emergency. Cool with lukewarm water and air on the way; ice-cold makes it worse.

Water and the small stuff

Multiple water stations, refreshed daily, because stagnant warm water goes undrunk. Shade in the yard, always. A paddling pool is genuine equipment, not a photo prop. And grooming matters: a matted coat traps heat against the skin. Keep double-coated breeds brushed and never shaved to the skin; the coat insulates against heat too.

One product note we repeat every summer: cooling mats and elevated beds genuinely help, but only if the animal chooses to use them. Place them where the pet already naps, not where they photograph well. In our test homes, the elevated cots in the shade got used daily; the fancy ones in the sun got ignored all season. Location beats technology, as usual.

How hot is too hot to walk a dog?

Use the pavement test: if you cannot hold the back of your hand on the asphalt for seven seconds, it burns paws. Walk early morning or late evening in summer.

What are signs of heatstroke in pets?

Heavy panting that turns weak, bright red gums, wobbliness, vomiting or collapse mean heatstroke, a straight-to-vet emergency. Cool with lukewarm water on the way.
