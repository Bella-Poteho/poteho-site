How a pet changes your mornings

There are people who wake up slowly, to sunlight and a moment of quiet reflection. I used to be one of them. Now I wake at 6:31 to a dog standing on my chest, breathing into my face with the urgency of a smoke alarm that loves me.

Owning a pet rewrites your mornings completely, and after years of this I have come to believe the rewrite is mostly an upgrade.

The new schedule

Pets do not respect weekends. The cat's breakfast is at six, weekdays and Sundays alike, and the dog's internal clock has an error margin of about four minutes. You will become a morning person or a very good negotiator. Most people become a morning person, grudgingly, then genuinely.

There is worse company to keep. The morning now includes a walk before screens, which is the habit every wellness book sells and every dog delivers for free. Cold air, quiet streets, a dog completely thrilled to be alive at an hour when you were previously a zombie. It is difficult to stay completely grim while holding a leash attached to that much optimism.

The small rituals

The cat's supervision of tooth-brushing. The dog's opinion of the coffee machine, which has been negative for six years and shows no signs of softening. The way both animals migrate to whichever corner of the kitchen the sun reaches first, like clockwork, while you make breakfast.

None of this shows up in the reasons people give for wanting a pet. Everyone says companionship, and they are right, but the daily texture of that companionship is oddly specific. It is mostly mornings. The evenings are for the couch; the mornings are for the contract.

There is research suggesting pet owners start the day with lower stress than non-owners, which my dog produced by standing on my bladder, but the finding holds. A creature that needs you before you have decided to need anything gets you moving, outdoors, on a rhythm.

I still have slow Sunday mornings sometimes, when the family is away and the pets are boarding. I sleep until nine. I do not like it. The 6:31 face is better.

How do pets change your morning routine?

Pets set a fixed schedule of feeding and walks at the same time daily, which gets you moving and outdoors before screens, often lowering morning stress.

Why do pets wake up so early?

Their internal clocks are precise and they do not observe weekends. Consistent feeding times train their wake-up, so steady routines make mornings smoother.
