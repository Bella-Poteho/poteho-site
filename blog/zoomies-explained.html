The 3pm zoomies, explained

The technical name is Frenetic Random Activity Periods, which scientists clearly named while laughing. You know it as the zoomies: the sudden full-speed gallop, tight corners, low slung turns, sometimes with a toy, sometimes with nothing but pure velocity.

Cats do the 3am version. Dogs do it after baths, at dusk, at the park gate, and, with great reliability, at around three in the afternoon.

What zoomies actually are

Energy discharge, mostly. A body that has been storing energy, from rest, excitement, or relief, hits a threshold and dumps it all at once. The zoomies are less a decision than a pressure valve.

Watch when they happen and the pattern makes sense. After a bath: relief and the weird sensation of being suddenly lighter. After a long day of being polite: the accumulated arousal of self-control finding an exit. Morning and evening zoomies track the natural activity peaks of a crepuscular species, the dawn-and-dusk schedule written into dogs and cats long before sofas existed.

Stress can also trigger them. A dog that zooms when guests arrive may be discharging nerves, not joy. Context tells you which. Loose body, play bows, happy face: fun. Wide eyes, frantic pacing before the run: that is anxiety looking for a door.

Should you do anything

Usually, no. Zoomies are normal, healthy, and self-limiting. The jobs of the owner are simply these: clear the runway, meaning nothing breakable or slippery on the route, and let it end on its own. Do not chase a zooming dog in a park; you will not win, and being chased reads as the best game ever invented.

If the zoomies are constant, that is information too. It usually means the dog's day is missing structured exercise or work, so the pressure valve opens hourly. More walk, more sniffing, a training session, and the eruption schedule calms down.

There is no product that replaces a zoomie, and we have never tried to make one. The closest we came was watching which toy a dog grabs before starting its lap. It is almost always the one it can carry at full speed, which is why our rope toys are sized for a sprinting jaw and not for the shelf. The zoomie picks the inventory.

What are the zoomies?

Zoomies (FRAPs) are sudden bursts of running and spinning, usually harmless releases of pent-up energy, often after baths, meals or excitement.

Should I stop my dog's zoomies?

Usually not; they are normal and brief. Keep the space safe. If they are frequent or destructive, more exercise or mental enrichment may help.
