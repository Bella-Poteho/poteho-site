Travel with cats: easier than you think

Cats have a reputation as terrible travelers, and it is deserved, in the same way a person would be a terrible traveler if every journey of their life went straight to the dentist.

Fix what the journey means, and most cats handle travel better than their owners do.

Weeks before

The carrier comes out of storage and becomes living room furniture. Feeding near it, treats in it, the door removed if it has one. A cat that naps in its carrier on ordinary Tuesdays has already won half the trip.

Do a rehearsal drive: five minutes, no destination, home again, treat. Then ten. The car stops predicting the vet.

If your cat is truly anxious, talk to the vet about it in advance. There are legitimate options, and the right one depends on the cat and the length of the trip. Do not improvise with human medication.

The day of

Feed light, hours before. Line the carrier with something that smells like the cat's usual spot, and bring a spare pad, because accidents happen on long days. Cover the carrier with a thin blanket once in the car; darkness settles most cats faster than anything else you can do.

Secure the carrier on the floor behind a seat or belted in, never loose on a seat and never in a cargo area. A frightened cat inside a loose carrier during a hard brake is the scenario to avoid.

For road trips, keep the carrier closed at stops. The classic lost-cat-at-the-gas-station story begins with an open door and a confident prediction that the cat would not dare. It dares.

Arriving somewhere that is not home

Set up one closed room first: litter, water, the carrier left open as a den, familiar blanket. Let the cat decompress there while the rest of the space settles. Most cats expand their territory within a day or two if the base camp felt safe.

And update the microchip details before any big move or trip. It costs nothing and it is the difference between a story with a bad afternoon in it and a story with a bad ending.

Our most-traveled office cat logs a few hundred kilometers a year between our sample rooms, asleep in the same worn carrier the whole way. She is not a brave cat. She is a well-briefed one. That is the entire difference, and it is available to any cat with a patient human and a carrier that means good news.

How do I travel with a cat?

Use a secure, familiar carrier with a soft pad, keep the cat indoors at stops, and acclimate it to the carrier and car with short practice trips beforehand.

Is traveling with a cat hard?

It is easier than many expect if you plan: a comfortable carrier, pheromone wipes, a quiet hotel room, and consistent feeding times reduce stress for most cats.
