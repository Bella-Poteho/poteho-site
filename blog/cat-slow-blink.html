The slow blink: how cats say I love you

There is a piece of cat language so simple that most people learn it by accident and so useful that I want to teach it to everyone: the slow blink.

You have seen it. The cat looks at you, eyes soft, and closes them slowly, holds a beat, opens again. Not a stare. Not a squint. A deliberate, unhurried close and open, like a tiny curtain acknowledging an old friend.

What it means

In cat, prolonged direct eye contact is a threat. Staring is what rivals do. So a cat that looks at you, deliberately relaxes its eyes, and closes them in your presence is saying: I could be on guard, and I am choosing not to be. Around you, my defenses are down.

Researchers tested this formally a few years ago. Cats shown a human slow blink were more likely to slow blink back and more likely to approach an extended hand afterward. The expression works across species lines. It is probably the only sentence you can say in cat.

How to say it back

Catch a calm moment. Cat across the room, relaxed, looking your way. Soften your own face, look at it with half-lidded eyes, close them slowly, hold a second, open slowly. Then look away. The look-away matters; it proves the whole thing was friendly and there is no follow-up stare.

Do not lean in, do not reach. Just the blink, then break eye contact. Do it a few times over a few days and watch what happens. Many cats will blink back. Some will get up and come over, because you just told them, in their own grammar, that you are safe.

It sounds like nothing until it works on a cat that does not come to people. Then it feels like a key.

We have a resident office cat who supervises sample arrivals, and new visitors are always surprised when she walks over to them and not the person waving toys at her. The ones she picks are almost always the quiet ones who happened to slow blink without knowing why. Cats keep their own census, and the slow blink is how you get counted as a friend.

What does a cat slow blink mean?

A slow blink is a cat's way of saying it feels safe and trusts you: it deliberately drops its guard. You can slow blink back to return the sentiment.

How do I slow blink at my cat?

Soften your face, close your eyes slowly, hold a beat, open slowly, then look away. Do not lean in or reach; many cats blink back or come closer.
